The pipeline

Ten stages, run on demand.

The same governed sequence runs whether the output is a blog post, a landing page, a newsletter or a white paper — only the brief and the format template change. Three separate optimization passes, because writing for Google and writing to be quoted by an LLM are no longer the same job.

Three separate optimization passes, then two governance gates. Nothing reaches your CMS carrying an unresolved red flag.

01

Parallel research

Two research agents run at once — industry and market on one side (trade press, regulatory signals, SERP gaps, competitor coverage), company and product on the other (features, use cases, customer outcomes, GTM positioning).

02

Topic bank, scored

100 deduplicated topics per run written into a live queryable datastore — each with brief, persona, buying stage, content type, SEO angle and priority score. A standing backlog, not a one-off list.

03

Selection & ranking

Topics get ranked against demand signals and funnel balance, so your program doesn't over-index on top-of-funnel explainers while the BOFU pages stay empty.

04

Drafting with citations

Full data-backed posts with key takeaways, inline citations and a source list. Unsourced assertions don't get quoted by AI search, so nothing ships unsourced.

05

Imagery generation

An on-brand hero image plus inline visuals generated with the post, so nothing ships as an unbroken wall of text and your team isn't hunting stock photography before publishing.

06

Humanization

The AI tells come out — uniform sentence rhythm, hedge stacking, hollow transitions, reflexive rule-of-three lists — rewritten toward specificity and varied cadence. Editorial work, not detector evasion.

07

SEO optimization

Classic search mechanics: target keyword and entity coverage, heading hierarchy, meta and title construction, internal linking, and schema. This is the pass that wins the click from a results page.

08

Straight answers

What everyone asks me before signing.

Answered the way this engine writes — direct answer first, detail after. That structure is what gets a page quoted in AI Overviews, so this section is also a working sample.

Does Google penalize AI-generated content?

No. Google's stated position is that it rewards high-quality content regardless of how it was produced, and penalizes content created primarily to manipulate rankings. The production method isn't the problem — unoriginal, unverified, unhelpful output is.

The risk sits in what most AI content programs skip: fact-checking, genuine research, original framing and demonstrable expertise. Those are exactly the stages I enforce before a draft reaches your CMS.

How do you humanize AI content?

By rewriting for the things models get uniformly wrong: sentence rhythm, specificity and commitment. I strip the AI tells — evenly-lengthed sentences, hedge stacking, hollow connectives, reflexive rule-of-three lists, generic examples — and replace them with concrete detail, varied cadence and actual positions.

It's editorial work, not detector evasion. The goal is copy your subject-matter expert would sign their name to.

How do you optimize content for AI search engines?

Three things on every piece: entity clarity, answer-ready structure and semantic completeness. Entity clarity means naming products, categories and concepts explicitly instead of relying on pronouns. Answer-ready structure means each section resolves one discrete question in a passage an LLM can lift cleanly. Semantic completeness means covering the adjacent questions a reader asks next, so the page is the sufficient answer.

Add verifiable cited claims and you have a page an AI system can quote and attribute with confidence.

What's the difference between SEO and AEO or GEO?

SEO optimizes to win a click from a results page. AEO (answer engine optimization) and GEO (generative engine optimization) optimize to be the source an AI system quotes when it answers directly — often with no click at all.

They overlap, but reward different things. Classic SEO rewards authority and keyword coverage; AEO additionally rewards extractability, citation quality and factual precision. The programs winning right now do both, which is why I build both in rather than selling AEO as an add-on.

How do you fact-check AI-generated content?

Every claim and statistic is extracted and classified as Verified, Flagged or Red Flag against its source. Verified claims carry a working citation. Flagged claims are ambiguous, dated or weakly sourced and get rewritten or re-sourced. Red flags are unsupported and get removed or replaced before approval.

I apply corrections upstream of your review — you're approving a post, not auditing one.
