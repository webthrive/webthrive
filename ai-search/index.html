AI Search

// 001 //

The Short Version

SEO isn't dead — it's holistic.

Rankings alone don't win markets anymore. Brands do.

In a world shaped by AI search, social feeds, and recommendation engines, growth comes from aligning distribution, brand, and user experience — not chasing algorithms in isolation.

// 002 //

My Core Belief

Brand > keywords.

People don't recommend URLs — they recommend brands. The companies that win are the ones that create memorable, repeatable touchpoints across channels, long before a buyer is ready to convert.

Search is no longer a single destination. It's an ecosystem — Google, YouTube, social, PR, podcasts, communities, and now LLMs.

My work lives at the intersection of all of them.

// 003 //

The Mental Models I Use

The 7 / 11 / 4 Rule

Buyers don't convert after one touch.

On average, prospects need:

  • • 7 hours of exposure
  • • 11 memorable interactions
  • • Across 4 different platforms

If your brand isn't intentionally showing up across channels, you're invisible — even if you rank.

Why Search Still Matters (Even as It Shrinks)

Yes, Google usage is declining.

But when compared to LLM-driven discovery, search is still one of the most scalable, defensible demand channels available.

The move isn't to abandon SEO — it's to diversify distribution while keeping search as a foundation.

Prompts Are the New Metadata

Average prompts are getting longer, more specific, and more contextual.

That changes how content should be structured:

  • • Clear positioning
  • • Strong semantic coverage
  • • Repetition of core ideas across channels

If you want AI systems to recommend you, you have to teach them who you are.

Distribution Is the Strategy

Content doesn't fail because it's bad. It fails because no one sees it.

I design distribution before creation:

  • • Social and community seeding
  • • Digital PR and earned media
  • • Podcasts and long-form conversations
  • • Video-first publishing

Why YouTube Is Non‑Negotiable

YouTube:

  • • Drives branded search demand
  • • Is heavily crawled by search engines
  • • Acts as a content amplifier for every other channel

One strong YouTube asset can become:

  • • Short-form clips
  • • Blog content
  • • Landing pages
  • • Sales enablement material

// 004 //

How I Treat Content

Content is a product.

That means it should be:

  • • Defensible
  • • Repeatable
  • • Built on proprietary insight

The formats that consistently work best:

  • • Data-backed stories and reports
  • • Opinionated listicles and rankings
  • • Video (long-form + short-form)
  • • Persona-specific landing pages
  • • Interactive and experiential pieces

Memorable beats polished.

// 005 //

Brand Is a Performance Lever

Brand isn't fluffy.

Strong brands:

  • • Increase branded search volume
  • • Lift non-branded rankings
  • • Lower CAC
  • • Improve conversion rates

Social, PR, and media coverage don't just drive awareness — they build semantic associations that influence both search engines and LLMs.

Show up consistently enough, and systems start repeating you.

// 006 //

SEO and LLMs Aren't the Same Game

Classic SEO and AI assistants don't reward the same signals.

  • • Backlinks still matter for Google
  • • Coverage, repetition, and authority matter more for LLMs

There's very little overlap today between:

  • • Top Google rankings
  • • ChatGPT-style recommendations

The opportunity is designing strategies that compound across both, instead of optimizing for one in isolation.

// 007 //

Measurement Has Changed

Traffic alone is a lagging indicator.

I track:

  • • Brand search volume
  • • Impressions and visibility
  • • Branded vs non-branded demand
  • • Engagement and recall signals

Search quality raters and AI systems both reward effort, clarity, and consensus.

// 008 //

How I Execute

I approach content like a product launch — not a publishing calendar.

Video leads, because it creates the strongest signal of authority, trust, and recall. Everything else follows.

Long‑form video becomes the source of truth:

  • • Narrative and positioning
  • • Language buyers actually use
  • • Objections, nuance, and context

From there, content fans out:

  • • Short‑form clips for social and distribution
  • • Transcripts and edits for blog and search
  • • Landing pages and persona pages for demand capture
  • • Sales and enablement assets

Content and distribution are planned together. Creation without amplification is wasted effort.

Insights from content performance feed back into:

  • • Product positioning
  • • Messaging and packaging
  • • What we double down on next

SEO isn't a silo. It's part of go‑to‑market.
